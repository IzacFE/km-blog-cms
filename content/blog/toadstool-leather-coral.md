Toadstool corals are leather corals from the genus *Sarcophyton*. They grow a thick stalk topped with a wide, mushroom-shaped cap, and when the polyps are open the whole cap looks soft and furry as it sways in the flow. Most toadstools are very hardy, which makes them one of the best first corals for a new reef tank.

## What Is a Leather Coral?

Leather corals are soft corals with no hard skeleton. Their tissue feels firm and rubbery, which is where the name comes from. The main groups are:

- **Toadstool (*Sarcophyton*)**: stalk with a mushroom-shaped cap
- **Finger leather (*Sinularia*)**: branching, finger-like growth
- **Cabbage leather (*Sinularia dura*)**: flat, wavy plates

## Common Toadstool Types

- ***Sarcophyton glaucum***: the classic tan or brown toadstool with long polyps; very tough
- **Green toadstool**: green-tinted tissue or polyps that glow under blue light
- ***Sarcophyton trocheliophorum* (trough coral)**: a folded, wavy cap with short polyps
- **Fiji yellow toadstool (*Sarcophyton elegans*)**: bright yellow-cream and very popular, but more demanding than other toadstools and benefits from regular feeding

## Care Requirements

### Lighting
- Low to moderate light, around PAR 100–200
- Very adaptable, but acclimate it slowly to new or brighter lights

### Water Flow
- Moderate flow
- Enough movement to keep the cap clean and let it shed properly

### Water Parameters
- Temperature: 24–26°C (75–79°F)
- Salinity: 1.025–1.026 SG (34–35 ppt)
- Alkalinity: 8–10 dKH
- Tolerates a wider range than most stony corals, but stability still matters

## The Waxy Shedding Phase

Every few weeks a toadstool may close its polyps, shrink, and look "dead" for a few days. This is normal. It is growing a thin waxy film over its surface, which it then sheds to clean itself of algae and debris. Moderate flow helps the film come off. Do not move or touch it during this time, and it will open again once it is done.

## Chemical Warfare

Leather corals release terpenoid compounds into the water to defend their space. In a mixed tank these chemicals can irritate or slow the growth of nearby stony corals. To keep things peaceful:

- Leave plenty of space around it, because the cap can grow wide
- Run activated carbon and change it regularly
- Keep up with regular water changes

## Placement and Growth

- Place it on the rockwork or lower in the tank
- It grows quickly and can become very large, so plan for the space
- It may lean towards the light; this is normal

## Propagation

Toadstools are easy to frag:
- Cut a piece off the edge of the cap, or cut through the stalk
- Attach the cutting to a plug with a rubber band or thread until it bonds
- Keep the frag in moderate flow while it heals

## Conclusion

Toadstools are forgiving, fast-growing, and full of movement. Give them space, run carbon, and expect the occasional shedding phase, and they will reward you with a big, eye-catching centrepiece for years.
