A reef tank is a small piece of the ocean at home: live corals, invertebrates, and fish living together in balanced saltwater. It takes more planning than a freshwater tank, but with the right equipment and some patience it is very achievable. This guide covers the essentials.

## Start With the Right Size

Bigger tanks are more stable. More water means temperature and parameters change more slowly, which gives you more time to fix mistakes. For a first reef, around 100–300 litres is a good balance between stability and cost. Use our [Tank Volume Calculator](/calculators/tank-volume) to work out your real water volume.

## Essential Equipment

- **Lighting**: corals need light to feed their zooxanthellae. A reef LED with a blue-heavy spectrum is the most common choice.
- **Water flow**: a return pump plus powerheads or wavemakers keep water moving and deliver food and oxygen. See the [Flow Calculator](/calculators/flow).
- **Filtration**: live rock and the bacteria on it do most of the biological filtering. A protein skimmer removes waste before it breaks down.
- **Sump (optional but useful)**: a tank below the display that holds equipment and adds water volume. See the [Sump Calculator](/calculators/sump).
- **Heater and cooling**: keep the temperature at 24–26°C. In a hot climate like Indonesia, a fan or chiller is often more important than a heater.
- **Auto top-off (ATO)**: replaces evaporated water with fresh RO/DI water so salinity stays stable. See the [ATO Calculator](/calculators/ato).

## Water: RO/DI and Salt

Always mix saltwater with RO/DI water, not tap water. Tap water carries phosphate, nitrate, silicate, and metals that feed algae and harm corals. Mix salt to around 1.025–1.026 SG (34–35 ppt). The [Salt Water Calculator](/calculators/salt-water) tells you how much salt you need.

## Cycling the Tank

Before adding any animals, the tank must "cycle". This is the nitrogen cycle:

1. Waste and food break down into **ammonia**, which is toxic
2. Bacteria convert ammonia into **nitrite**, which is also toxic
3. Other bacteria convert nitrite into **nitrate**, which is much less harmful

Cycling usually takes 3–6 weeks. Test regularly, and only add your first livestock once ammonia and nitrite both read zero.

## Stocking Slowly

- Start with hardy animals: a clean-up crew (snails, hermit crabs) and easy fish
- Add easy corals first, such as soft corals, zoanthids, or mushrooms
- Add one or two animals at a time so the bacteria can keep up
- Quarantine new fish whenever possible

## Testing and Maintenance

Key parameters to test:
- Alkalinity, calcium, and magnesium (the "Big Three")
- Nitrate and phosphate
- Salinity and temperature

A simple routine:
- **Daily**: check temperature, equipment, and that the animals look normal
- **Weekly**: test the main parameters, clean the glass, and empty the skimmer cup
- **Every 1–2 weeks**: change 10–20% of the water

Use the [Water Chemistry Calculator](/calculators/water-chemistry) to check your test results against recommended ranges.

## Patience Is the Key

Most reef tank failures come from rushing: adding animals too early, adding too many at once, or chasing perfect numbers with big adjustments. Stable parameters matter more than perfect ones. Go slow, test often, and enjoy watching your reef grow.
