Goniopora, often called "Gonio" or flowerpot coral, is a large-polyp stony (LPS) coral with long, flower-like polyps that extend from a round skeleton and sway constantly in the flow. Each polyp ends in a ring of 24 small tentacles, which gives a healthy colony a fluffy, bouquet-like look.

## A Coral With a Reputation

For many years Goniopora had a reputation as one of the hardest corals to keep. Many wild-collected colonies slowly starved and died within months. Today things are different: aquacultured Goniopora, many of them from Indonesia, adapt much better to aquarium life, and many reefers now keep them successfully for years. It is still not a beginner coral, but it is no longer impossible.

## Care Requirements

### Lighting
- Moderate light, around PAR 100–200
- Too much light can make the polyps retract

### Water Flow
- Moderate, indirect flow
- The polyps should sway gently, not get blasted flat

### Water Parameters
- Temperature: 24–26°C (75–79°F)
- Salinity: 1.025–1.026 SG (34–35 ppt)
- Alkalinity: 8–10 dKH
- Calcium: 400–450 ppm
- Magnesium: 1300–1400 ppm
- Nitrate: a small measurable amount, around 2–10 ppm
- Phosphate: around 0.03–0.1 ppm

Goniopora does poorly in ultra-low nutrient water. Do not chase zero nitrate and phosphate in a tank where you keep one.

## Feeding

Feeding is a big part of keeping Goniopora healthy:
- Fine particle foods such as phytoplankton and zooplankton
- Amino acid supplements
- Feed small amounts often rather than large amounts at once
- Turn the flow down briefly while feeding

## Placement

- Many keepers place it on the sand bed or low on the rockwork
- Keep it away from aggressive corals that can sting the polyps
- Choose its spot well and avoid moving it often

## Warning Signs

- **Polyps not extending for days**: check light, flow, and parameter swings
- **Tissue receding from the skeleton**: often caused by starvation or unstable alkalinity
- **Polyps shrinking over weeks**: usually not enough food or nutrients

## Conclusion

Goniopora rewards patience. Start with an aquacultured colony, keep your parameters stable, leave some nutrients in the water, and feed regularly. When it is happy, few corals can match its constant, flowing movement.
